  - title: "QUALIRAD"
    subtitle: "Quality Control of Radiology Equipment · HC-UFPE"
    kicker: "HEALTH · INNOVATION · HOSPITAL DAS CLÍNICAS DA UFPE"
    period: "2026 — present"
    status: "Active"
    description: >
      Brazil's health regulatory agency (Anvisa), through RDC 611/2022 and Normative Instructions 90 to
      97, requires radiology services to periodically test equipment such as X-ray machines, mammography
      systems and CT scanners, but does not define how the results should be recorded. In practice, they
      end up scattered across spreadsheets, printed forms and loose reports, which hinders audits,
      control of test frequency and any analysis of how the equipment evolves over time. QUALIRAD
      translates the regulation's requirements into checklists standardized by modality and test type,
      automatically checks whether each measurement is within tolerance, generates PDF and CSV reports
      and works fully offline as a progressive web application (PWA). The checklists were built and
      reviewed with medical physicists and clinical engineers, and the tool, tested at HC-UFPE, is moving
      on to validation and deployment in other federal university hospitals.<br><br>
      I took part in the meetings with the HC-UFPE team to gather requirements and led the choice of the
      technologies used in the project. Together with the group, we refined the solution up to the version
      presented to the hospital, and I now lead the development of new versions with the course's current
      students, steering the project toward deployment in every university hospital in the federal network.
    role: "Full stack developer · Main author of the paper at the Brazilian Congress on Biomedical Engineering (IEEE), 2026"
    highlights:
      - "Standardizes the records of the quality control tests required by Anvisa, simplifying audits and test frequency control."
      - "Automatic compliance checking of measurements, with 41 automated tests covering tolerance edge cases."
      - "Test history as the basis for tracking equipment degradation and anticipating end of service life."
      - "Tested at HC-UFPE, with deployment planned in other federal university hospitals."
    tags:
