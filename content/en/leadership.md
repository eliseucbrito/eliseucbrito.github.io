CInCoders is the development division of the Systems Management at the UFPE Center for Informatics. Alongside institutional demands, we started a separate university extension project this year that functions as a software factory: undergraduate students develop real applications for non-profit organizations, with production-quality standards.

As co-coordinator and technical leader of the extension project, my responsibility goes beyond code. I manage a team of more than 10 students, define project architectures, perform code reviews, and track deliveries from end to end. I also structured an agile methodology adapted to the academic rhythm — because making software work within a university requires adjustments that ready-made methodologies don't cover.

We currently maintain active projects with partner organizations such as <a href="https://instagram.com/gris.solidario" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent underline underline-offset-2 decoration-accent/40 hover:decoration-accent transition-colors">Gris</a>, <a href="https://instagram.com/casadoestudantepe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent underline underline-offset-2 decoration-accent/40 hover:decoration-accent transition-colors">Casa do Estudante de Pernambuco</a>, and <a href="https://instagram.com/comunidadevilaarraes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent underline underline-offset-2 decoration-accent/40 hover:decoration-accent transition-colors">Associação de Moradores da Vila Arraes</a> — institutions that wouldn't have resources to hire a development team and now count on real, custom-built software.

The practical result is a team that delivers functioning software to organizations that wouldn't have resources to hire a development team.
